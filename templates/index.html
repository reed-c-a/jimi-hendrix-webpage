Jimi Hendrix Timeline

Here's a rough timeline of the personal and musical life of Jimi Hendrix.

Early life and music exposure

November 27, 1942

Johnny Allen Hendrix is born in Seattle, Washington to parents James Allen "Al" Hendrix and Lucille Jeter.

1946

Johnny Allen Hendrix's parents change his name to James Marshall Hendrix in honor of his father Al and his late brother Leon Marshall.

December 17, 1951

Hendrix's parents are divorced, with Al gaining custody of him and his older brother Leon. Hendrix spends the mid-1950s at Horace Mann Elementary School in Seattle, carrying a broom with him and pretending it was a guitar.

1957

Hendrix helps his father with a side job cleaning an old woman's home and finds a ukulele with a single string in the garbage. He learns single-note songs by ear, like Elvis Presley's "Hound Dog."

February 2, 1958

Hendrix's mother, Lucille, dies to a spleen rupture caused by liver cirrhosis. Al refuses to let the brothers attend her funeral, instead giving them shots of whiskey to "cope with loss."

A 15-year-old Hendrix acquires his first acoustic guitar for $5 and plays for hours a day, listening to the likes of Muddy Waters, B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf, and Robert Johnson. He soon formed a short-lived band called the Velvetones, after which Al reluctantly got him an electric guitar. Later, Hendrix joined an unnamed band and played a few gigs, but was fired for showing off.

Joining the military

May 31, 1961

Hendrix enlists in the military after being given an ultimatum between military service or prison time for riding in stolen cars. He completed eight weeks of basic training at Fort Ord, then was assigned to 101st Airborne Division and stationed at Fort Campbell, Kentucky. He wrote back home declaring his displeasure with training, pleading his father to send him his guitar.

November, 1961

A fellow serviceman Billy Cox notices Hendrix's playing at an army club. The two jammed, with Cox on bass guitar. They periodically played with other military musicians as a loosely formed group called The Casuals.

June 29, 1962

Hendrix is granted a general discharge under honorable conditions. This was likely connected to a report written by his Platoon Sergeant, James C. Spears, claiming that he had "no interest whatsoever in the Army." Hendrix later claimed that he was given a medical discharge after breaking his ankle during a parachute jump.

After the military

September, 1962

Hendrix and Cox form a band, the King Kasuals, in Clarksville, Tennessee. The band's second guitarist, Alphonso Young, would sometimes play the guitar with his teeth. Hendrix decided to learn to do the same. The group eventually found their way into Nashville's Jefferson Street, which was filled with prestigious blues musicians. Hendrix played with his own band here, as well as other venues that were part of the Chitlin' Circuit throughout the South as a backing musician for artists like Ike and Tina Turner.

Around February, 1964

Through a series of connections, Hendrix lands a role in the Isley Brothers' backing band. He toured with them throughout most of 1964 and recorded a single with them, "Testify."

February, 1965

Hendrix starts touring with Little Richard's band, the Upsetters. He recorded a single with Richard, which underperformed as Richard's popularity was dwindling at the time.

July, 1965

Hendrix appears on the television show "Night Train", performing the song "Shotgun" in Little Richard's band. This is the earliest known footage of Hendrix performing. Hendrix was fired from the band later that month for his antics.

After playing in a few more bands, like Curtis Knight and the Squires, as well as attempting to front his own band, Jimmy James and the Blue Flames, Hendrix meets Linda Keith, who was Keith Richards' girlfriend. He is referred to Chas Chandler, the bassist for The Animals (of "House of the Rising Sun" fame), who was interested in forming and managing a band. Chandler scouts band members for a new group that could highlight Hendrix's talents. These members end up being bassist Noel Redding and drummer Mitch Mitchell. The members unite over their shared interest in rhythm and blues. The three members become known as the Jimi Hendrix Experience after Chandler convinces Hendrix to change the spelling of his first name.

The Jimi Hendrix Experience

October 1, 1966

Chandler brings Hendrix to the London Polytechnic, where the band Cream is scheduled to perform that night. Hendrix asks their guitarist, Eric Clapton, if he could perform a few songs. Clapton obliges. Hendrix performs Howlin' Wolf's "Killing Floor," including some of his tricks like playing with his teeth and behind his back. Clapton would later say that "my life was never the same again" after that performance.

October 13, 1966

The Jimi Hendrix Experience play their first show: a brief, 15-minute act at the Novelty in Evreux as a supporting act for Johnny Hallyday.

October-November, 1966

The Experience record their first song, "Hey Joe". The band's first original song, "Stone Free," is recorded shortly thereafter. Later, when performing a show at the Big Apple in Munich, Hendrix smashes his guitar in a panic when trying to escape the crowd, which created an explosion of sound. The audience believes this to be part of the show and reacts uproariously. This later becomes a permanent fixture of Hendrix's shows.

November, 1966

At the Bag O' Nails nightclub in London, the Experience perform for a crowd that included Eric Clapton, Paul McCartney, John Lennon, Mick Jagger, and Jeff Beck. They are all reportedly shocked by what they're hearing, and this performance lands Hendrix his first interview with the publication Record Mirror. They predict that Hendrix would take the industry by storm.

December 16, 1966

The single "Hey Joe" backed with "Stone Free" is released through Polydor Records. The former soon enters the UK charts and later peaks at number 6. Next year, the band releases "Purple Haze" and "The Wind Cries Mary", which perform even better on the UK charts.

March 31, 1967

The Experience perform at the London Astoria. At the end of the 45-minute show, Hendrix sets fire to his guitar after having some lighter fluid placed onto it beforehand. London's press goes wild, as expected.

Are you experienced?

May 12, 1967

The Jimi Hendrix Experience's first LP, "Are You Experienced?" is released in the UK. It spends 33 weeks on the charts, peaking at number 2 (number 1 at the time was "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" by The Beatles). The album includes standard blues songs like "Red House", hard rockers like "Purple Haze" and "Foxey Lady", sweet ballads like "May This Be Love" and "The Wind Cries Mary", and highly experimental tracks like "Third Stone from the Sun" and the title track. The album is later released in the United States on August 23 of that year through Reprise Records.

June 4, 1967

The Experience opens a show at London's Saville Theatre with a performance of "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" by the Beatles, which had come out only 3 days earlier. McCartney and Harrison were in the audience, with the former counting it as "one of the great honors of my career."

June 18, 1967

The Experience perform at the Monterey Pop Festival after the organizers of the event are strongly encouraged to include them by McCartney. Hendrix shows up in very exotic clothing and performs several covers and songs from their debut album. At the end of the show, Hendrix sets his guitar on fire, which becomes one of the most iconic moments in the history of rock music. By the end, the audience can't believe what they've seen or heard.

Axis: bold as love

Before December, 1967

The recording sessions for "Axis: Bold As Love" take place. The release is almost delayed after the master tape for the A-side of the record was left by Hendrix in the back seat of a London taxi. Hendrix, Chandler, and the mixing engineer Eddie Kramer remix most of these songs in one night, but the song "If 6 Was 9" has to be recovered from a tape that Redding has.

The cover for the album is produced, depicting the members of the Experience as various avatars of the Hindu diety Vishnu. Hendrix later complained about the cover, saying "I'm not that kind of Indian" (Context: Hendrix was alledgedly of Cherokee descent).

December 1, 1967

The Experience's second LP, "Axis: Bold As Love" is released in the UK. It peaks at number 5 on the UK charts. In February of the following year, the album reaches number 3 in the US charts. The album contains a lot more studio experimentation than the band's previous album, including stereo panning and the first recorded instance of stereo phasing.

Electric Ladyland

December 20, 1967

The recording sessions for the Experience's third and final LP, "Electric Ladyland," begin.

April, 1968

The recording sessions for the album are moved to the newly opened Record Plant Studios in New York. Around this time, Chandler gets frustrated with Hendrix's studio antics (including inviting many friends and guests into the studio and demanding many retakes) and cuts ties with him and the band. Redding also forms his own band, Fat Mattress, around this time, leaving him unable to record often and requiring Hendrix to record many of the bass parts himself.

October 25, 1968

Electric Ladyland is released, becoming the Experience's most successful album, and their only to reach number 1 on the US charts (it reached number 6 in the UK). It includes a cover of Bob Dylan's "All Along the Watchtower," which was Hendrix's most successful song and remains his most popular on streaming. It is by far their longest album, at 1 hour and 15 minutes. Critics generally agree that it is the band's best work.

January, 1969

The Experience perform on BBC's "Happening for Lulu" show. They play Voodoo Child, then break out into a rendition of Cream's "Sunshine of Your Love." They continue playing it for so long that the producer tries to get them to stop, but they continue playing. They are kicked off and Hendrix is alledgedly banned from working with the BBC.

February-April, 1969

Tensions within the band reach a tipping point, with Redding being frustrated by Hendrix's unpredictable work ethic and total creative control.

June 19, 1969

The Experience play their last show together: the Denver Pop Festival. Prior to the show, Redding is informed by a journalist that Hendrix had announced that he was fired and replaced by Billy Cox (mentioned in the military section). During the event, tear gas is deployed on the crowd by police. The next day, Redding leaves the band to pursue a solo career. He later claims to have liked Hendrix, but not Mitchell, and that he and Mitchell only hung out together because they were both British.
